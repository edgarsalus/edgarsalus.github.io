Finding exoplanets with fewer featuresFinding exoplanets with fewer features

How much accuracy do machine-learning models lose when half their input features are removed? A study on NASA Kepler data.

Illustration, not Kepler data

Role
Independent researcher, sole author
Timeline
Jan 2023 – Aug 2024
Team
Solo project
Tools
Python, scikit-learn, TensorFlow, XGBoost, pandas
  • ~10,000Objects from NASA’s Kepler cumulative catalog
  • 3Models compared on three feature sets
  • ≈72%Decision-tree accuracy with the top 50% of features (≈97% with all)
  • >95%Neural-network accuracy on every feature set

    01

    The question

    When a planet crosses its star, the star dims slightly. Kepler produced thousands of candidates, and the models that classify them differ in how much data they need.

    Question

    How much accuracy does each model lose when it gets fewer features?

    02

    Approach

    1. 01

      Data

      ~10,000 objects

      Tabular data from the Kepler cumulative catalog.

    2. 02

      Rank

      XGBoost

      Used only to rank feature importance.

    3. 03

      Subset

      Three sets

      All features, the top 75% and the top 50%.

    4. 04

      Compare

      Three models

      Decision tree, random forest, neural network.

    03

    Results

    Takeaway

    Tree models depend on a broad feature space; neural networks re-⁠weight their inputs and stay robust.
